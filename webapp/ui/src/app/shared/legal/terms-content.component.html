4. How a round works

This is a short description. The Platform shows the current details of each round.

  • A pool opens at regular intervals. While it is open, you can commit SOL and name a Solana memecoin that the Platform accepts.
  • The pool closes when its time runs out or when it reaches its cap. SOL you commit cannot be withdrawn, including before the pool closes.
  • After the pool closes, a draw sets how much of the pool goes to buying each named coin. The draw uses verifiable randomness from Switchboard. If that service does not respond, we may complete the draw with a fallback so the round can finish. The more SOL stands behind a coin, the closer its share tends to stay to that amount. A small amount can end up with a larger share, a smaller one, or nothing.
  • The pool, less the platform fee, is transferred to a buyer wallet operated by the team. The buyer wallet buys the coins on-chain in small batches over roughly one hour. Every buy is a public transaction.
  • The tokens bought for a coin are sent to the people who backed that coin, in proportion to the SOL each of them committed.
